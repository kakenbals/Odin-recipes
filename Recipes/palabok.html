Pancit Palabok (Filipino Rice Noodles with Pork and Shrimp Sauce)

What is Palabok?

Pancit Palabok is a Filipino rice noodle dish with a rich pork and shrimp sauce, similar to a ragu, and is also often called Pancit Luglug
or Pancit Malabon I explain the subtle differences below. The noodles are simply boiled in water, topped with palabok sauce, and garnished
with smoked fish, eggs, and crumbled chicharron.It is a popular dish to serve for celebrations and special occasions, and one of my personal favorites.

Its eclectic mix of ingredients is a good example of the different influences on Philippine cuisine. The noodles, of course, came from the
Chinese, and the achiote tree, from which annatto seeds are sourced to give the palabok sauce its color, was introduced by the Spaniards.
Combine this with very traditional ingredients such as rich seafood, and you have a uniquely Filipino noodle dish.

Ingredients

  • 225g Bihon (thin rice noodles)
  • 1/2 lb Shrimp (peeled and deveined, save the heads/shells)
  • 1/2 lb Ground pork
  • 2 cups Annatto water (2 tbsp annatto seeds soaked in 2 cups warm water, then strained)
  • 1 Shrimp bouillon cube
  • 3 tbsp Fish sauce
  • 1 medium Onion, minced
  • 5 cloves Garlic, crushed
  • 4 tbsp Cornstarch dissolved in 1/2 cup water
  • 2-3 Hard-boiled eggs, sliced
  • 1/2 cup Chicharon, crushed
  • 1/4 cup Smoked fish (tinapa) flakes
  • 1/4 cup Toasted garlic
  • 1/4 cup Green onions, chopped
  • Calamansi wedges

How to Cook Palabok?

  1. Prep the noodles: the rice noodles in cold water for 30 minutes, then boil them for 5 to 10 minutes until tender. Drain well and spread them evenly across your serving platter.
  2. Boil the shrimp:Drop the peeled shrimp into boiling water for 2 minutes until pink, then remove them and set them aside for the final topping.
  3. Make the shrimp stock: Boil the remaining shrimp heads and shells in 3 cups of water for 10 minutes, then strain the liquid into a bowl and discard the shells.
  4. Brown the meat and aromatics: 2 tablespoons of oil in a pan, sauté the ground pork until lightly browned, then stir in the garlic and onions for 2 minutes until fragrant.
  5. Simmer the sauce base: Pour the fish sauce, annatto water, and your prepared shrimp stock into the pan, drop in the shrimp bouillon cube, and simmer for 10 minutes.
  6. Thicken the sauce: Slowly pour the cornstarch mixture into the pan while stirring constantly, allowing it to bubble gently until it transforms into a thick, glossy orange sauce.
  7. Assemble and garnish: Pour the hot sauce directly over the bed of noodles, arrange the shrimp and egg slices on top, and scatter the chicharon, tinapa flakes, toasted garlic, and green onions over everything before serving with calamansi.
